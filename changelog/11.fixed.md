The LLM usage total showed prompt tokens, the metrics plot pinned its axis to
ten seconds, and the transcript was rewrapped on every token.
Quitting the dashboard in the bot's terminal after the bot had finished hung,
and so did Ctrl-C after it. Quitting now stops the bot in view, with the bot's
own log handlers kept instead of replaced by a plain one, and a bot shut
down from outside closes the dashboard.
With the dashboard in the bot's terminal it showed the bot as DISCONNECTED,
and the LISTENING, THINKING and SPEAKING pills never lit up.
When Pipecat ended the user's turn at a pause and the user went on before the
bot spoke, the turn's `→ llm` line quoted only the last part, as if the LLM
had missed the rest. It now shows every part the LLM got.
The live response time counted from the moment the VAD decided you had
stopped, about its `stop_secs` (often 200 ms) after you actually went silent,
so it read less than the measured answer that replaced it. Tail now places
speech and silence where they happened using the VAD's own `start_secs` and
`stop_secs`, and the live time matches Pipecat's measurement.
The conversation could stop short of the latest turn when a turn grew without
a new message, such as a tool call's line while the bot thinks or a resize.
While following, the latest line now always stays in view, and the key line
shows `f follow` in amber whenever following is paused.
A murmur the VAD heard but the transcription dropped moved the bot to
THINKING about five seconds later, when Pipecat ended the empty turn, and the
clock counted up for an answer that never came. Tail now keeps the bot
LISTENING after a turn with nothing heard, and starts the wait only when the
bot takes the turn up (as Pipecat does when the murmur cut the bot off). When
you then speak, the turn starts over: its start, length and events in the
turn inspector count from what you said, and the murmur's timeout is left out
of how soon your turns end.
With `TailObserver()` added to a worker without `worker=`, the dashboard
called the bot "pipeline" instead of the worker's name. The observer now finds
its worker as the pipeline is set up.
With a `TailObserver` on its own, the Pipeline view showed an empty runner and
bus, as if something had broken. Its workers, jobs and bus panels now say
they need a `TailWorker`, and the worker is listed without a runner Tail can't
see.
Once the call was over, the voice timeline and the speech lanes kept scrolling
with the clock until they were empty. They now stop where the call ended.
After the bot left, the Pipeline view kept showing the frame rates of the
pipeline's last quarter second, as if frames were still moving. They now drop
to zero when the pipeline finishes or the bot goes away.
In a long call the Logs view and the bus feed stopped showing new records
once Tail had kept as many as it holds (5,000 log lines, 2,000 bus messages).
The voice timeline only redrew when something in it moved, so a change such
as the call ending while you looked back showed only once something moved.
With several pipeline workers reporting through one worker, every log line
reached the Logs view once per pipeline. The process's logs are now forwarded
once.
In the light theme, log messages, worker names and startup figures were drawn
in the dark theme's light colors and could hardly be read. They now follow the
theme, and the log and bus feeds are redrawn when it changes.
The call time in the status line took the bot's clock from this machine's, so
it was off by however far the two clocks were apart, as with a bot on another
machine.
`--json` could leave turns out of a long session: it remembered printed turns
by Python object ids, which the turns the session no longer keeps hand on to
new ones.
After stepping through issues or a search in Live, switching worker with `w`
kept the other worker's "issue 2 of 5" in the conversation's title. The title
now works out where the selected turn is each time it is drawn.
A wait the bot never answers stops counting after 30 seconds, but only once
something had asked what the bot was doing: until then the screen kept
redrawing as if the bot were still thinking. Reading the state no longer
changes it.
With the dashboard in the bot's terminal, what the bot printed while it was
open was thrown away, so when the call ended before you quit, the terminal never showed
the worker shutting down. It is kept and shown once the dashboard closes.
Under load, Live could stop following the call: after a turn grew, the view
checked once, on the next refresh, whether to move, and when the turn had not
been laid out yet it stayed put until the next message. It now moves whenever
the conversation's size changes, which is known only after layout.
Under a development runner, which starts the bot again for every call, a new
call opened a second dashboard on the same terminal while the first stayed
open, and quitting the first froze the second. The calls now take turns in one
dashboard, which switches to the new call when the previous one is over.
With Pipecat 1.3 to 1.7 the bus feed stayed empty after startup, and the Tail
worker no longer heard the runner: bus messages have names only from Pipecat
1.8, and forwarding them failed on reading one.
A message the dashboard failed on stopped it for good in the bot's terminal,
which looked frozen while the bot went on, and made `pipecat tail`
reconnect. The message is now skipped, with an error in Logs saying which.
The dashboard in the bot's terminal was a fork of the bot. It held the bot's
port and sockets, so a dashboard left behind kept the port taken and ignored
`kill`, and it could freeze on a lock one of the bot's threads held as it
forked. It is now `pipecat tail`, started for you in a process of its own and
connected to the bot like a remote one, and it quits when the bot is gone.
