Tail is rebuilt for Pipecat 1.x and works with every 1.x release, from 1.0
with `PipelineTask` to the latest with `WorkerRunner`, and is ready for 2.0.
`TailObserver` runs a private `RTVIObserver` (it isn't one itself, so the
bot keeps its own RTVI) and Pipecat's latency, turn tracking, startup timing,
error, function call and service metrics observers where the bot's Pipecat
has them, falling back to RTVI where it doesn't. It
reads the newer RTVI messages (`user-input` with chat, backchannels, tone and
speaker, the raw text messages, `bot-output` delivery, raw VAD, mute and LLM
markers), reports frame flow between processors and its own audio levels,
sends the LLM context as it changes (messages, system instruction, tools and
image thumbnails), and forwards structured log records. `TailServer` is a worker on a
`WorkerRunner` that reports workers, jobs and bus traffic, and `TailRunner`
opens the app in the bot's terminal.
The turn inspector has three pages: the turn, the LLM context it saw (what's
new marked, images as thumbnails) and every event of the turn. `s` saves a
session report as Markdown and JSON, `--report` writes one on quit, and
`--json` streams the session as JSON lines without the UI. `--target` sets the
response time latency colors are graded against. The layout adapts to the
terminal from a 48-column side pane to a full screen: a turn's notes go under
what was said, columns give way by priority so no number is cut, log messages
wrap, commands break where a shell can read them, and past turns in Live take
a line each for the user and the bot. It redraws only what moves.
Tail spots early ends of turn: Pipecat ending your turn at a pause, the LLM
starting on what it heard, and you going on before the bot speaks. The turn
is flagged with what the LLM got of it, the end is marked on the voice
timeline, the session counts them, the turn inspector explains them, and
reports list them. The demo has one.
The observer reports raw VAD from the VAD's own frames, so the voice timeline
and speech lanes show it with every Pipecat 1.x, not only from 1.8.
`examples/bot.py` runs with a `TailObserver` with `TAIL_RUNNER=observer`.
When a call ends, or the bot goes away mid-call, a dialog says so and how it
went: its length and turns, response times, early ends, interruptions and
errors. `n` and `p` close it and step back through what went wrong.
