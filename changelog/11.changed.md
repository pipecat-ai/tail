The app is redesigned around time. Always on top: the bot's state
(LISTENING, THINKING, SPEAKING), the voice-to-voice time in large digits that
count up while the bot thinks, where the current turn's time is going as it
happens, and on wide terminals how the session is going. Live, the new home
screen, shows a voice timeline with both sides' audio, raw VAD, turn ends,
response brackets, backchannels and interruptions over the conversation,
every turn, following the latest like a page: the text stays put until it
reaches the bottom, then jumps so the latest line is in the middle. `n` and `p` step through the turns where
something went wrong (an early end, a cut, a slow answer, an incomplete turn,
an error) and `/` searches what was said; while you look back, and after the
call, the timeline keeps only its lanes. Color follows the conversation: teal while the bot
listens, violet while it thinks, pink while it speaks; green, amber and red
only grade or flag. Panels are sections under a rule instead of boxes, each
turn opens with a rule carrying its number, flags and time, and what is
normal stays quiet: no legends (help explains the marks), no "turn complete"
on every turn, no repeated frame rates, no empty jobs table, and tags are
words rather than filled blocks.
Metrics shows each service metric as a row: a sparkline of recent
measurements in the service's color, then the last, the median and the worst,
with what each metric means. Usage (tokens, characters, audio) follows. The
chart is gone, and with it the `textual-plotext` dependency.
Latency (a waterfall per turn, where the time goes, the
distribution and speech lanes), Pipeline (flow, startup, workers, jobs and
bus), Metrics and Logs hold the detail, Enter opens a turn inspector, and a
waiting screen explains how to attach a bot. Tail has its own dark and light
themes. `pipecat tail --demo` plays a scripted session without a bot.
Recording and replaying sessions (`--save`, `--replay`, `--speed`) is gone.
Python 3.11 and Pipecat 1.0 are now the minimums.
Audio levels are Tail's own, measured as the bot plays its audio, instead of
RTVI's loudness.
In Logs, `n` and `p` select the next or previous error line and pause
following, with "error 2 of 5" in the title, and `e` shows the latest error
from anywhere, instead of a notification. Every processor error now has its
own line in the logs, since Pipecat logs them as warnings, if at all.
